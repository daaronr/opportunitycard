OpportunityCard

A proposal to let people prove a low income to a seller and get a lower price. I've worked on it on and off since 2008. This page collects the public papers, posts and discussion in one place.

Comments welcome. Select any passage to annotate it with Hypothes.is, or open the panel at the right edge. Or email daaronr@gmail.com.

The idea

Governments know people's incomes. Sellers mostly don't, so they decide who gets a discount using cruder signals: age, student status, location, purchase history. An OpportunityCard would let a customer who chooses to certify that their income is below a threshold. The seller gets a yes-or-no answer, not tax records, and can offer a posted discount to people who show the card.

The appeal is that it could reduce consumption inequality through markets rather than through taxes and spending. The catch is that the welfare effects are theoretically ambiguous. A verified low-income group can expand trade and reduce the hassles and deliberately worse "budget" versions firms use to keep better-off buyers from taking discounts. It can also let firms extract more surplus or raise the standard price. That's why I've argued for testing it before anything larger.

Where it stands now

The OpportunityCard, ten years on A reassessment and research agenda (July 2026). The verification tools the 2015 proposal lacked now exist, and related discounts already run at scale, such as UK social broadband tariffs checked against benefit records and Brazil's automatic enrollment in its electricity social tariff. My current view: pin down the remaining theoretical problem and audit the available administrative data before anyone proposes a general rollout.
